PERSPECTIVE · GLOBAL MARKETS

Born Global, Built Local

Globalization is not ending; it is being rewired. Supply chains, data rules and capital flows are fragmenting—and the founders who master that complexity will build the most resilient companies of the decade.

Mahayana ResearchOctober 2026 · 9 min read
IN THIS PERSPECTIVE
  1. A rewired world
  2. The new playbook
  3. What it means for capital
  4. Questions for founders

For a generation, the formula for a global company was simple: build in one place, sell everywhere, and let open markets do the rest. That formula is breaking. Tariffs, export controls, data-localization laws and industrial policy now shape where products can be made, sold and serviced.

Yet demand has never been more global. A software tool launched in Shanghai can find customers in São Paulo within a week. A hardware startup in Shenzhen can serve hospitals in Europe. The tension between global demand and fragmented rules is the defining operating challenge for ambitious founders today.

01

Not deglobalization—re-globalization

Trade and investment are not disappearing; they are rerouting. Manufacturing is spreading across more countries. Regional hubs in Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Mexico and Eastern Europe are gaining weight. Capital is following technology, energy and supply-chain security.

The map has not shrunk. It has become more detailed.

For founders, this means the cost of global expansion has risen, but so has the reward for doing it well. Companies that can navigate compliance, localize operations and earn trust in multiple markets build advantages that are difficult for fast followers to copy.

02

The born-global playbook

01

Global from day one

Design product, pricing and architecture for multiple markets early, rather than retrofitting a domestic product later.

02

Local where it matters

Data residency, local entities, local partners and local leadership turn regulatory friction into a barrier for others.

03

Resilient supply chains

Dual sourcing and modular manufacturing reduce exposure to any single trade route or policy decision.

04

Compliance as capability

Export controls, sanctions screening and privacy law are not afterthoughts. Teams that build them into operations move faster with confidence.

03

What it means for capital

Investors must underwrite geography as carefully as technology. That means understanding which markets a company can legally serve, how its supply chain would respond to a policy shock, and whether its governance can earn the confidence of customers and regulators in different jurisdictions.

It also means being present. A partner with networks across Asia, North America and Europe can open doors, anticipate rule changes and help founders sequence expansion. In a fragmented world, the value of a truly global partnership rises.

In a fragmented world, trust is the scarcest cross-border asset.
04

Five questions we ask founders

  1. 01Which markets can you serve today, and which could close to you tomorrow?
  2. 02How would your business respond to a sudden tariff or export restriction?
  3. 03Where must data, people and production be local—and where can they be shared?
  4. 04Who on your team has built in more than one regulatory environment?
  5. 05How does each new market make the next one easier to enter?

Global ambition, local discipline.

The next generation of category leaders will not choose between global scale and local trust. They will build both from the start. Rooted in Asia and connected to the world, we are committed to helping founders turn complexity into lasting advantage.
