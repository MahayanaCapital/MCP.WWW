From Sick Care to Health Care: Prevention Becomes Investable

Most health systems are built to treat illness after it appears. Continuous data, AI and new care models are making it possible to act earlier—and creating one of the most important opportunities in healthcare.

Mahayana ResearchOctober 2026 · 9 min read
IN THIS PERSPECTIVE
  1. The shift upstream
  2. Four building blocks
  3. Making prevention a business
  4. Questions for founders

Healthcare has long been paid to respond. A patient feels unwell, visits a clinician, receives a diagnosis and begins treatment. By then, many chronic conditions have been developing silently for years.

Aging populations and rising chronic disease are making that model unsustainable. At the same time, the tools to intervene earlier—continuous sensors, affordable diagnostics, AI that can read patterns across them—are finally mature enough to use at scale.

01

The shift upstream

Prevention is not new as an idea. What is new is the ability to measure the body continuously and act on what is found. Wearables track sleep, heart rhythm and activity every day. Home tests and lower-cost lab panels make regular screening practical. AI can turn these signals into personalized, timely guidance.

The most valuable moment in medicine is the one before the patient becomes a patient.

Consumers are moving first. Many now want to understand and manage their health proactively, and are willing to pay for it. Employers, insurers and governments are following, as the cost of late intervention becomes impossible to ignore.

02

Four building blocks

01

Continuous measurement

Sensors and diagnostics that capture meaningful signals in daily life, not just during clinic visits.

02

Clinical-grade insight

Models that separate signal from noise and translate data into guidance clinicians and individuals can trust.

03

Engagement that lasts

Products people return to for years, because behavior change—not one-time insight—drives outcomes.

04

Care integration

Connections to physicians and care teams so early findings lead to real action rather than anxiety.

03

Making prevention a business

The challenge has always been economics. Prevention creates value over years, while budgets are set over quarters. The companies that succeed will prove outcomes with evidence, align with payers and employers who capture the savings, and build consumer brands strong enough to sustain direct demand.

Trust is the foundation. Health data is deeply personal, and recommendations carry real consequences. Founders must hold themselves to clinical standards of evidence, privacy and transparency—especially when selling directly to consumers.

In preventive health, evidence is the brand.
04

Five questions we ask founders

  1. 01Which outcome do you change, and how will you prove it?
  2. 02Who pays—consumers, employers, insurers—and why will they keep paying?
  3. 03How do you keep people engaged after the novelty fades?
  4. 04How do your findings reach a clinician when action is needed?
  5. 05How do you earn and protect trust in the data you hold?

Health, not just care.

The next era of healthcare will be measured by the illnesses that never happen. We are looking for founders who combine rigorous science with consumer-grade experiences—and the patience to prove that prevention works.
